# Research report

**Question:** What caused the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023?

## Summary

The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023 was precipitated by a large‑loss sale of its Treasury bond portfolio, which sparked a rapid and massive withdrawal of deposits, ultimately leading to a bank run and the bank’s failure. [^1]

## Triggering Event: Loss‑Making Bond Sale

On March 8, 2023 SVB announced it had sold more than $21 billion of securities at a substantial loss and was borrowing $15 billion while also planning a $2.25 billion treasury‑stock sale to raise cash. [^1]

The disclosure of this loss‑making bond sale immediately raised concerns about the bank’s liquidity and prompted depositors to withdraw funds. [^1]

## Rapid Deposit Withdrawals and Bank Run

Following the announcement, customers withdrew $42 billion in a single day, overwhelming the bank’s cash reserves and triggering a classic bank run. [^1]

## Contextual Risk Factors

Regulatory data showed that 89 % of SVB’s deposits were uninsured as of December 31, 2022, creating a heightened liquidity vulnerability that amplified the impact of the bond‑sale announcement. [^2]

## Limitations

- The findings do not detail the precise timing of the bond‑sale loss relative to market conditions.
- No direct evidence is provided on internal risk‑management decisions preceding the sale.
