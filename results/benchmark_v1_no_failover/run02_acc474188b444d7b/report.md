# Research report

**Question:** How does the EU AI Act classify high-risk AI systems, and when do its obligations take effect?

## Summary

The EU AI Act defines high‑risk AI systems based on their role as safety components of regulated products or their inclusion in Annex III, with specific exemptions, and lists eight sector categories; the compliance obligations for these systems become legally binding on 2 August 2026. [^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Classification Criteria for High‑Risk AI

An AI system is classified as high‑risk if it is intended to be used as a safety component of a product, or is itself a product covered by the EU legislation listed in Annex I and therefore requires a third‑party conformity assessment. [^1]

Additionally, any AI system listed in Annex III is deemed high‑risk unless it can demonstrate that it does not pose a significant risk to health, safety or fundamental rights, or that it does not materially influence decision‑making outcomes. [^1]

Annex III AI systems that perform profiling of natural persons are automatically classified as high‑risk, regardless of other considerations. [^1]

## Specific High‑Risk Applications and Sectors

Annex III identifies eight high‑risk categories: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services \(including credit scoring and insurance\), law enforcement, migration, and justice. [^3]

These sectors are reinforced by the Act’s broader list of high‑risk areas, which also mentions migration, asylum and border control alongside the categories above. [^2]

## Effective Date of High‑Risk Obligations

The compliance obligations for high‑risk AI systems become legally binding on 2 August 2026, when the Act’s general application date and the Article 50 transparency duties take effect. [^4]

## Limitations

- The findings do not specify how the exemption assessment for Annex III systems is verified in practice.
- No information is provided on transitional provisions for systems already on the market before the 2 August 2026 date.
