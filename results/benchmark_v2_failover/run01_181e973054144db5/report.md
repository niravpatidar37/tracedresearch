# Research report

**Question:** What caused the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023?

## Summary

The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023 was precipitated by a rapid liquidity crisis triggered by the sale of a large Treasury bond portfolio at a loss, followed by a bank run that drained deposits and forced the bank to raise emergency capital. [^1]

## Liquidity Risk and Asset Composition

Prior to its failure, Silicon Valley Bank held approximately $212 billion in total assets, with its principal subsidiary holding about $209 billion. A significant portion of its loan portfolio—about $6.7 billion—was concentrated in early‑ and growth‑stage companies whose repayment depended on exit events such as IPOs or sales. [^2][^3]

## Regulatory Warnings

The California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation issued a supervisory letter on November 17 2022 highlighting liquidity risk factors for the bank, and a May 31 2022 letter addressed governance and risk management concerns. [^4]

## Triggering Events and Bank Run

On March 8, 2023, SVB announced the sale of over $21 billion of securities, a $15 billion borrowing, and an emergency sale of treasury stock to raise $2.25 billion. The announcement, coupled with investor warnings, triggered a bank run, with customers withdrawing $42 billion by the next day. [^1]

The sale of its Treasury bond portfolio at a large loss raised depositor concerns about liquidity, directly contributing to the run and the bank’s collapse. [^1]

## Limitations

- Limited data on the exact timing of deposit withdrawals relative to the bond sale.
- No detailed breakdown of the bank’s risk management response to the liquidity warnings.
