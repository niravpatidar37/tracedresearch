# Research report

**Question:** What is the current schedule and cost estimate for the ITER fusion reactor?

## Summary

The ITER fusion reactor is progressing ahead of its baseline schedule, with key construction milestones already surpassed and a projected completion of research operations in 2034 and deuterium–tritium operation in 2039. The total cost estimate has risen from the original $20 billion to about $25 billion due to recent overruns. [^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Construction Schedule

Site preparation began in 2010, the Tokamak Building basemat was poured in 2013, and the assembly phase started in 2020. Major components such as the first toroidal field coil were installed in 2021 and the cryostat lower cylinder in 2022. [^1]

Since January 2024 the schedule performance index has remained above 1.0, indicating that construction is ahead of the baseline plan. [^2]

By the end of June 2026, 192 Level 1 schedule milestones had been achieved against 188 planned, and six of nine vacuum‑vessel sector modules were in the tokamak pit. [^2][^3]

## Limitations

- Limited data on future milestones beyond 2039.
- No detailed breakdown of cost components beyond the total estimate.
