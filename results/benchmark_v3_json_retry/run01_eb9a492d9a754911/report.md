# Research report

**Question:** What caused the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023?

## Summary

The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023 was precipitated by a combination of high exposure to early‑stage company loans, a deposit base dominated by uninsured deposits, and a liquidity shock triggered by a large loss on its Treasury bond sale, which led to a rapid bank run. [^1][^2][^3]

## Financial Profile Prior to Collapse

Silicon Valley Bank held approximately $212 billion in total assets, with its principal subsidiary reporting about $209 billion in assets. The bank’s loan portfolio included roughly $6.7 billion to early‑ and growth‑stage companies, loans that were likely to be repaid only upon an exit event such as an IPO or sale. [^1][^4]

## Regulatory Risk Indicators

Regulatory reports as of December 31 2022 highlighted that 89 % of the bank’s deposits were uninsured, a significant concentration risk. Despite this, the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation assessed the bank as being in “sound financial condition” before March 9 2023. [^2]

## Triggering Events and Bank Run

On March 8, the bank announced the sale of over $21 billion of securities, a $1.8 billion loss on the sale, and a borrowing of $15 billion, followed by an emergency sale of treasury stock to raise $2.25 billion. The loss on the Treasury bond portfolio raised depositor concerns about liquidity, sparking a rapid withdrawal of funds. [^3][^5]

By March 10, the rapid withdrawals had left the bank unable to generate sufficient cash to meet depositor needs, leading to its collapse. [^5]

## Limitations

- Limited detail on internal risk management decisions leading up to the sale of securities.
- No information on the exact timing of deposit withdrawals relative to the bond sale.
