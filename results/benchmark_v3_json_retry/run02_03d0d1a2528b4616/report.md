# Research report

**Question:** How does the EU AI Act classify high-risk AI systems, and when do its obligations take effect?

## Summary

The EU AI Act classifies high‑risk AI systems as those used in sensitive domains such as biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment and worker management, access to essential services, law enforcement, migration, and the administration of justice, or that act as a safety component of a regulated product. The obligations for these systems become enforceable on 2 August 2026. [^1][^2]

## High‑risk AI system categories

High‑risk AI systems are defined as those employed in sensitive domains including biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment and worker management, access to essential services, law enforcement, migration, and the administration of justice, or that function as a safety component of a regulated product. [^1]

## Obligations for providers

Providers must establish a quality management system, maintain documentation and logs, and conduct a conformity assessment before market release. [^3]

They must issue an EU declaration of conformity, affix the CE marking, and comply with registration and accessibility requirements. [^3]

They are responsible for taking corrective actions and demonstrating compliance to authorities upon request. [^3]

- Evidence does not detail the specific content of the quality management system or the exact procedures for corrective actions.
